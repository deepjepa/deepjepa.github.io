Overview

Spend computation where it can change the decision.

World-model planners usually scale outward by rolling farther, sampling more trajectories, or optimizing longer. DeepJEPA introduces a complementary axis: adaptive recurrent depth inside each imagined transition.

A weight-tied joint-embedding predictive world model learns whether another recurrent update is worth computing for each candidate and rollout step. Useful refinement concentrates at decision-critical events rather than being applied uniformly to every transition.

Method

A budgeted computation process inside every transition.

DeepJEPA ties parameters across recurrent updates, predicts whether to continue, and returns the selected latent state to the unchanged CEM planner.

1

Imagine

Roll candidate actions through latent space using the same outer planning budget.

2

Refine selectively

Spend extra recurrent depth only when the continue head predicts another update is worthwhile.

3

Rank and act

Return the selected predictions to CEM, where changed costs can alter elite membership and the executed action.

Results

Better planning without uniformly deeper rollouts.

Across five visual-control settings, DeepJEPA improves or matches the strongest fixed-depth planner while staying close to the shallow endpoint.

Analysis

Depth follows physical interaction.

Refinement rises sharply at PushT contact onset and during sustained Cube Triple contact, despite training without contact labels. The learned computation policy tracks changes in local dynamics rather than rollout position alone.

Interaction-aligned depth concentrates computation at the transitions most likely to alter planner rankings.
